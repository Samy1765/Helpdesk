# Reporting a Suspicious Email

## Warning signs
- Urgent pressure: "your account will be closed today", "payment overdue", "CEO needs gift cards".
- The sender's display name looks familiar but the address is from an outside or misspelled domain.
- Links whose real address (shown when you hover over them) does not match the text.
- Unexpected attachments, especially .zip, .html, .iso or Office files that ask you to "Enable content".
- Requests to approve an MFA prompt or read out a code.

## What to do
1. Do not click links, open attachments or reply.
2. Use the "Report phishing" button in Outlook. This sends the message to the Security Team and removes it from your mailbox.
3. If the message seems to come from a colleague or supplier, confirm with them by phone or Teams using contact details you already have, not those in the email.

## If you already clicked or entered your password
1. Change your password straight away from a different, trusted device.
2. Raise a Security ticket marked high priority and say exactly what you clicked or entered, and when.
3. Do not switch off the laptop; disconnect it from Wi-Fi and the network cable so the Security Team can examine it.
Reporting quickly matters far more than any embarrassment. Nobody is disciplined for reporting a mistake.
